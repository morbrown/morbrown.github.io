Research

Pronominal syntax and interpretation

Much of my current work concerns pronominal syntax, typology, and interpretation. In particular, I am working to understand the fine-grained articulation of the pronominal spine and the possible pronouns that can be built from that spine. With those ingredients fixed, we can begin to systematically derive their interpretation(s). In doing so, I investigate questions at the syntax-semantics interface regarding the role of narrow syntactic dependencies in binding, the interpretation of morphosyntactic features like person at LF, and consequences of decompositional approaches to pronominal structure for semantic composition.

In one project, I explore a structural reduction account of the null pronoun that participates in obligatory control in Uyghur. That work is currently in progress as a manuscript, but you can read more about it by viewing the handout for a talk I gave at the Stony Brook SynFl reading group.

In a second project, I argue that Agree is not involved in binding the long-distance reflexive in Uyghur. This work will appear soon as a TuPlus proceedings paper, and until then you can access the handout for the corresponding conference talk.

Experimental and corpus investigation of syntactic variation and change

I am also curious about how our theoretical commitments about grammatical structure interact with the empirical reality of synchronic variation and diachronic change, at the level of both individual speakers and speaker communities. In addition, I am interested in how diverse methodologies, including experimental and corpus studies, can inform our understanding of those phenomena. In an ongoing project with Arto Anttila and Brandon Papineau, we probe variation in nominal case in a set of embedded environments in contemporary Finnish, and are able to demonstrate that it is the consequence of a change in progress. We are currently assembling our findings into a full-length manuscript, but until then you can read more about this project by viewing our AMC Symposium poster here and our project visualizer here.
